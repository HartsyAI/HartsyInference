# MoE speed on an A40: HartsyInference against llama.cpp, 2026-10-11

Four MoE GGUFs, one RunPod A40 (48 GB, CUDA 13), the same prompt, sampler and server protocol for both engines. This is the
speed half of the program described in [MOE_PARITY_VS_STRATA.md](../../docs/MOE_PARITY_VS_STRATA.md); the architecture of what
changed is in [MOE_ARCHITECTURE.md](../../docs/MOE_ARCHITECTURE.md) ("Device-resident routed stage"). The results below come in two
rounds: the first (PR #367, alpha.334) and a follow-up round (PRs #369 and #372, alpha.337-338) re-measured at the end.

## Headline

- **Prefill is at or above llama.cpp on all four models**, from 1.00x (OLMoE) to 1.47x (Mixtral at 32K context).
- **Decode, final round, hartsy + F16 KV (the like-for-like column: llama.cpp's cache is F16 by default, ours is F32 unless
  `vram.kvF16` is set):** Granite 1.43-1.45x, OLMoE 1.10-1.13x, Qwen3-30B-A3B 0.97-0.98x at 4K and 0.88-0.93x at 32K, Mixtral 0.96x at 4K and
  0.95-0.97x at 32K. With the default F32 cache Qwen3 and Mixtral are 0.91-0.94x at 4K and 0.68-0.76x at 32K.
- Before this work the same models were slower than llama.cpp by 6-14x in prefill and 2-8x in decode (4K cell, same machine, against the
  llama.cpp rates in the tables below): Qwen3 prefill 265 and decode 18.8 tok/s (14x and 8x), Mixtral 325 and 29.5 (6x and 2x), OLMoE 1484
  and 60 (8x and 5x), Granite 1001 and 36 (13x and 6x).

What moved (4K, hartsy F32 KV unless noted): Qwen3-30B-A3B decode 129 to 138 tok/s (F16 KV 137 to 144); Mixtral 51 to 56 (F16 KV 53.5 to
58.8); OLMoE 232 to 251 (F16 KV 301 to 340); Granite 267 to 289 (F16 KV 299 to 314). Prefill is unchanged.

Greedy outputs against the previous `main` on the same prompts (200 tokens, A40; Granite, OLMoE, Qwen3-30B-A3B): the text matches for the
first 150-260 characters and then takes a different continuation. Looked at directly, the divergence points are near-ties between two
equally good phrasings (a markdown link against its plain text; "mentioned reading" against "mentioned the 'Add a model' section"), and both
continuations stay coherent. The cause is a different float summation order in the rewritten GEMV; the fusions in #372 are bit-identical to
the launches they replace. `tests/regression-ab.sh` was not run; this interleaved comparison stands in for it.

## What was slow and what fixed it

Each item was found from a measurement: the engine's per-op profiler for the first pass, then a CUPTI kernel trace of the real server (the
container does not allow `ncu`).

| Finding | Evidence | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Decode launched ~750 kernels per token from the host and read expert ids back | eager decode 18.8 tok/s on Qwen3 | expert-indexed `dp4a` GEMVs that read the expert id from device memory, device routing and combine: the whole MoE stage sits in the CUDA graph |
| Prefill ran one small GEMM per expert | 265 tok/s Qwen3 | dispatch by expert, one batched grouped GEMM per projection over dequantized stacks cached per layer |
| Prefill attention was the scalar kernel | 35 tok/s Qwen3 at 32K | FlashAttention-2 style tensor-core kernel (`mma.sync m16n8k16`, grouped-query, causal) |
| Decode attention read each KV head once per query head | 4K decode attention 2.2 ms of 9 ms | grouped-query flash-decoding: one read of K/V per KV head serves its whole group |
| Every request spent ~330 ms in graph capture | `decode_ms` fixed cost of 375 ms; trace showed 9 ms of kernels vs 12.5 ms per token through the API | a quadratic `List.Contains` over thousands of expert tensors in the weight-preload pass is now one pass (capture 350 ms to 17 ms). Qwen3 4K decode 80 to 125 tok/s |
| Mixtral prefill 1000 tok/s | trace: 0.96 s of F16-to-F32-to-BF16 cast kernels per request, grouped GEMM at ~34 TFLOPS | dequantize straight to BF16 in place; plain per-expert GEMMs when experts average 384 or more rows. 1000 to 2260 tok/s; Qwen3 prefill 2850 to 3850 from the cast fix alone |
| Sampled decode lagged greedy by 6% | `lm_topk_f32` 550 us per step on one block | two-stage top-k across 128 blocks (~55 us); sampled decode now tracks greedy |
| 32K decode attention at 58% of bandwidth | trace: 7.9 ms of 15.5 ms per step | next-tile prefetch, one-wave split sizing, F16 tiles kept F16 in shared memory: 6.4 ms |
| RMSNorm and Q8 quantize launches | 0.9 ms of a 9 ms Qwen3 step | register-resident norm+quantize kernels, bit-identical to the old ones; round 2 adds wide norms, a folded residual add, a full-width QK-norm+RoPE+scatter kernel and a Q8_1 copy written by the attention combine |
| Q4_K expert and dense GEMVs at 55-80% of bandwidth (3060 micro-benchmark) | Mixtral down Q4_K 55%, Qwen3 down Q4_K 61% | one 16-byte load per lane for both nibble planes, gate/up in one pass, split-K for long-K down projections: Mixtral down Q4_K 93%, Qwen3 down Q4_K 80% (Q6_K short-K rows stay at 66-83%) |

## Correctness and regression checks

- GPU test lane on an RTX 3060 (the 4090 was left to Swarm): 751 of 757 CUDA tests pass in one process. Five failures are CUDA
  initialization errors that appear only when the whole project shares a process and pass in isolation; one
  (`CudaExpertCacheTests.PinnedExpertsAreNeverEvicted_AndUnpinnedOnesAre`) failed identically on `main` and was a stale test, fixed in #368.
  New tests cover the indexed and grouped MoE stages (including the dense per-expert path), FlashAttention-2, grouped-query decode,
  F16 scatter, the fast norms (bit-identical to the old kernels), the fused decode ops, the GEMV ground truth, the device sampler's
  distribution against the host chain, and top-k.
- Dense LLM regression against `main` on the final round-1 commit, greedy, 96 tokens, RTX 3060: Qwen3-4B, Mistral-7B, Gemma-2-2B,
  Llama-3.2-1B, Phi-3-mini, Qwen2.5-0.5B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-1.5B, Gemma-3-1B, Granite-3-2B. Six produce byte-identical text. Qwen3-4B,
  Llama-3.2-1B and Qwen2.5-0.5B stay identical for 70-95% of the text and then take a different, coherent continuation. Each fused kernel
  changes float summation order slightly, and every variant of the build (any knob off, graph decode off) yields a different but valid
  greedy path on those three, as the old build does between graph and eager decode. Decode tok/s, best of two runs, old build then new
  build: Qwen3-4B 69.8 to 77.5, Mistral-7B 54.6 to 58.9, Llama-3.2-1B 133.5 to 146.8, Granite-3-2B 93.5 to 108.8, Phi-3-mini 81.6 to 82.1,
  Gemma-2-2B 87.6 to 87.1. The three smallest models read lower, within the old build's own run-to-run spread but all on the low side:
  Qwen2.5-0.5B 163.0 (152.3 on the second run) to 149.8 (132.7), DeepSeek-R1-Distill-1.5B 133.1 to 126.6, Gemma-3-1B 98.1 to 91.9. At that
  size a step is a few hundred microseconds and the graph launch gaps dominate; this is not chased here.
- Image models: after alpha.334 was deployed to the live Swarm, Krea2-Turbo ran 4.36 s (4.62 s before; gate under 6.5 s) and Z-Image-Turbo
  2.67 s (unchanged; gate 3.2 s), engine-internal times, with both images checked against their prompts. The later alpha.335-338 changes
  have not been deployed or re-checked there.

## Where it still falls short

- **Decode with the default F32 KV cache.** Qwen3-30B-A3B and Mixtral are 0.91-0.94x at 4K and 0.68-0.76x at 32K; with the F16 cache that
  llama.cpp runs, 0.96-0.98x at 4K for both and 0.88-0.97x at 32K. Flipping the engine's default changes numerics for every model, so it is
  a separate decision.
- **Expert GEMV bandwidth.** Q6_K rows with short K reach 66-83% of peak (three layouts were tried and were slower or flat); the quantize
  before the down projection and the MoE router launches are unfused. About 1.2 ms of every Qwen3 step on the A40 is gaps between the
  ~750 graph nodes.
- **Vulkan.** Not optimized for MoE and not measured on the pod (no Vulkan ICD in the container). The loader crash that stopped MoE models
  from loading on Vulkan is fixed (#371); on the RTX 3060 a dense 1B model then ran about 7x slower than CUDA (median) and granite-1b MoE
  about 18x slower. [VULKAN_MOE_PLAN.md](../../docs/VULKAN_MOE_PLAN.md) has the measured baseline and the port plan.
- The first temperature-0.0 round of a cell includes a cold request, so prefill in that row reads lower than the 0.7 rows.

## Reproduce

The scripts are in [2026-10-11_moe_perf_a40/scripts/](2026-10-11_moe_perf_a40/scripts/): `pod_up.py` rents the A40 and runs `bootstrap.sh` (llama.cpp at the pinned
commit, the Strata harness, the engine clone, the model downloads with checksums); then `scripts/pod-build-ref.sh <name> <ref>` builds an
engine, `mk-final.sh <name>` writes the dispatcher and per-model configs, `run-final.sh` runs the interleaved matrix and `make_report.py`
turns the harness summaries into the tables above. `trace-decode-long.sh` and `libcuptitrace.so` produce the kernel traces.
